# **Subsidized Home EV Charger in Vijayawada & Visakhapatnam: What the Official Support Actually Covers in 2026**

The short answer is not flattering, but it is accurate.

There isn’t any official Andhra Pradesh or central-government subsidy for a private home EV wallbox in Vijayawada or Visakhapatnam. Where I do find support in the official documents I have reviewed is public charging infrastructure. Andhra Pradesh’s[ Sustainable Electric Mobility Policy](https://nredcap.in/PDFs/2024/Andhra_Pradesh_Sustainable_Electric_Mobility_Policy_4_0.pdf) provides 25% capital subsidy to public charging stations and [PM E-DRIVE ](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2290696&lang=1&reg=22)provides ₹2,000 crore for EV public charging stations on a pan-India basis. That is the real picture of policy.

But that doesn’t mean home charging isn’t important. It just means you have to stop confusing subsidy with convenience. Most EV owners will still find the best daily-use setup is[ a home charger](https://spiderenergy.in/blog/ev-ready-homes-india-whole-energy-decision-2026) but the money support is aimed at the public network around your home, not the private wallbox bolted to your wall. Another reason to think home charging is[ a registration and installation questio](https://www.apcpdcl.in/faqs.php)[n](https://nredcap.in/EVApplication.aspx), not a direct subsidy question: NREDCAP’s charging [infrastructure portal ](https://nredcap.in/EVApplication.aspx)lists captive, private and public charging as separate categories for Andhra Pradesh.

## **What the official policy actually subsidizes**

Andhra Pradesh has a simple policy of 25% capital subsidy on the cost of charging stations excluding land, electricity connection, DTR and civil costs, for public charging stations. That is not a rebate for your house. It’s infrastructure support to chargers serving the public.

“PM E-DRIVE” is equally clear. The scheme has allocated ₹2,000 crore for EV public charging infrastructure and as on 01.07.2026, the Ministry of Heavy Industries said ₹689 crore has already been approved for 6,562 chargers across 3 OMCs and 9 States. The extension was also[ notified up to 31.03.2028](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2214039&lang=1&reg=3). The scheme is real, it is funded and it is live.[ Just not a home-charger subsidy.](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2290696&lang=1&reg=22&)

## **What a home EV charger actually is**

A home charger is not a mini highway fast charger.  That's a bad thought model.

Spider Energy’s current home-charging stack says its home line-up includes Spider Mini (3.3 kW) and Spider Lite (3.3 kW), and its AC range also includes Spider Smart (7.4 kW) for stronger residential or office use. The Har Ghar page says that the program is based on 3.3 kW single phase chargers and a standard home electrical connection. That means the proper home charger is an AC wallbox, not a DC fast charger.

### **Table 2: Charger size versus use case**

| **Charger size** | **Best fit** | **Why** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **3.3 kW** | Overnight home charging, apartments, city EVs | Lowest friction and matches the current Har Ghar home model. |
| **7.4 kW** | Faster home top-ups, larger EVs, more active users | Better for stronger AC home-charging support. |
| **11 kW** | Three-phase homes and vehicles designed for higher AC input | Useful only when the house and vehicle both support it. |
| **22 kW+** | Commercial and fleet use | Not the default home scenario. |

If your EV supports AC home charging, a home charger is a good idea. If you’re not, then you’re not in the right category of product. The charger should fit the vehicle, not vice versa.

## **What installation really requires**

A good home charger installation is not just a product purchase. That is a site decision.

The charger has to be compatible with the AC charging support of the EV, the property has to allow safe electrical connection and the installation has to respect the load capacity of the building. The Har Ghar home-charging model is based on a[ 3.3 kW single-phase charger](https://spiderenergy.in/), deliberately low-friction for regular home use. That is the size most private residential installs require.

### **Table 5: Home-charger installation checklist**

| **Checkpoint** | **Pass condition** | **Why it matters** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vehicle support | The EV supports **AC home charging**. | Otherwise the home charger is the wrong tool. |
| Connector fit | The vehicle and charger inlet are compatible. | No match means no useful installation. |
| Site readiness | The property can support a safe electrical connection. | Installation quality matters more than marketing. |
| Power level | **3.3 kW** is okay for overnight use; **7.4 kW** if the car and house support it. | Power level drives convenience, not subsidy. |
| Charging expectation | Home charging should be treated as overnight or slow charging. | This is not a fast-charging substitute. |

That is the reality. If you want a faster turn-around, you need a different charger size and a stronger electrical setup. If you want subsidy, that is the public-charging policy lane.

### **2) What do the official policies in Andhra Pradesh actually subsidize?**

They subsidise public charging infrastructure.  As per the Sustainable Electric Mobility Policy 4.0 of Andhra Pradesh, the public charging stations are provided with a 25 percent capital subsidy on the cost of the charging station excluding land, electricity connection, DTR and civil costs. PM E-DRIVE also has a specific budget for EVPCS for the support of public charging stations on a pan-India basis. That means the state and central governments are assisting the charging network to grow but not directly pay for a private home wallbox. Such is the true picture of policy. If you want the convenience of home, daily, install the charger privately. If your aim is subsidy, then the public charging route is supported.

### **5) Is a 3.3 kW charger enough for home use?**

Yes, for many families. The current Har Ghar style home setup uses 3.3 kW single phase chargers for regular home connections and this is enough for overnight charging or slow daily top-ups. It’s not a fast charger, and that’s the point. It's meant to be practical, and low-friction, not heroic. If you drive every day but park for long hours, 3.3 kw is usually enough. If you want quick charging, and your EV and electrical infrastructure allow for it, then a 7.4 kW wallbox might be a better option. Wishful thinking is not the answer, the correct answer depends on the vehicle and the property.

### **6) What is the difference between home charging and public charging in this context?**

Home charging is a private convenience asset.  The subsidised infrastructure is public charging. AP policy and PM E-DRIVE documents are for public charging stations, not a wallbox in a homeowner’s garage. That means the home charger is still relevant, but it’s a purchase you make and install yourself. Public chargers, in contrast, are what the government is actively helping to pay for to improve city coverage and highway coverage for everyone. If you are doing a comparison of the two, don’t compare convenience to subsidy. In subsidy compare to subsidy, convenience compare to convenience. They're not the same category.

### **7) Does PM E-DRIVE help home EV owners at all?**

Indirectly, Yes. The plan is intended for public charging points, but it’s still helpful for EV owners at home, as it makes city charging less of a pain, and highway charging less of a gamble. The EVPCS framework of the PM E-DRIVE covers charger categories at government premises, [public-sector locations](https://spiderenergy.in/blog/hotels-ev-charging-infrastructure-india), city sites and highways and has already approved funding for thousands of chargers. That means even if you pay privately for your home charger, the network around it is getting better. For the average homeowner, that's the real value: less dead space, better public backup, and less range anxiety.

### **8) What should a buyer in Vijayawada or Visakhapatnam do first?**

Check the site, then check the car's charging spec. If the EV supports AC charging then a home charger is a no brainer. If the property can accommodate a safe connection, then a 3.3kW or 7.4kW wallbox is usually the way to go. If you really want government support, look at the public charging lane. That is where the subsidies are. Don’t count on a private home rebate that official documents do not clearly provide for. The reasonable way is simple - a private home charger for the convenience of everyday life, and public subsidised charging for urban and highway backup.
