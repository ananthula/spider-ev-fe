# **What Makes a Great EV Charging Location?**

A 150 kW charger on a quiet service road may sit idle, while a 60 kW unit at a food plaza two kilometres away runs with queues on a Sunday evening. Hardware is much the same. The site isn’t. A great EV charging location for investors isn’t made by a pretty canopy. It is the intersection of demand [and dwell time and power and permission](https://evunlock.com/ev-charging-stations/).

India's public network has expanded quickly. As of mid-2026, Lok Sabha figures put the number of public stations at 52,718, of which 16,561 have fast-charging capability, while about 91 per cent of national highways are within 50 km of a charger. That growth conceals a brutal divide. Many sites still have thin average utilisation. Fleet contracts and corridor hubs with amenities can run several times hotter. Location is the variable that determines which side of that split you buy.

**Quick Answer Box**

The EV charging location that is visible is great, easy to get into, electrically ready and has drivers that will stay long enough for your charger speed. In India that typically means highway plazas and fuel retail for DC fast charging and malls, offices, [hotels](https://spiderenergy.in/blog/hotels-ev-charging-infrastructure-india) and housing for AC destination charging. Select the pedestal after you select the site you want.

## **What Makes a Great EV Charging Location in India?**

Five filters separate investable sites from decorative ones.

**Demand that is already moving.** Count registered EVs and daily traffic in the catchment, not slogans about future uptake. A practical planning rule operators use is to look for a meaningful EV population within about 10 kilometres in cities, or for corridor traffic that has to stop anyway. Demand for four-wheelers is concentrated in IT corridors, premium housing, logistics belts and intercity highways. Two and three wheeler density clusters around markets, metro last mile points and delivery kitchens. Don’t mix those use cases on the same bay design.

**Dwell time that matches charger speed.** It's this mistake that kills more models than tariff choice. A highway driver will give you 20 to 40 minutes. That requires 50-150 kW DC, preferably CCS2. An office or residential parker will give you hours. That draws 7-22kW AC. In the middle is a shopping trip and often requires a mix. If speed and stay don't match up, either the driver leaves early or the bay is blocked.

**Grid that can actually feed the site.** Land rent is less important than three-phase supply, transformer headroom, stable voltage and a path for a dedicated EV meter. In many Indian cities the civil work is easy but the DISCOM time line is not. Beautiful plot and weak feeder, stranded asset.

**Visibility and access.** Drivers follow by sight and by app. Sessions are lost to the next pin on the map for sites that sit behind a boom barrier with no signage or require a U-turn across a highway. Utilisation shows operating details like lighting, turning radius for CCS cable reach and protection from ICE vehicles parking in EV bays.

**A reason to stop besides the plug.** Toilets, shade, food, security and retail turn a charge into a planned stop. Highway rest stops and malls understand this. Not isolated plots.

The context is that [policy density targets still matter](https://savekar.com/guides/ev-charging-station-business-plan-location-strategy-and-return-on-investment-timeline). The guidance from Ministry of Power has always suggested a public station in a 3 km by 3 km urban grid and availability of charging on both sides of the highways at prescribed intervals. Those rules govern coverage. They don’t guarantee your cash flow personally. [Coverage sites and profitable sites are overlapping sets](https://www.nrdc.org/sites/default/files/location-everything-ev-issue-brief-20200127.pdf), not identical sets.

## **How to Select an EV Charging Location**

**How to select ev charging location** is a question that entails a diligence process, not a site visit with a tape measure.

Begin with a corridor or catchment thesis. Examples: Hyderabad ORR to Shamshabad, IT park cluster, mall with weekend dwell, fuel retail outlet on NH-65, fleet depot with overnight parking. Write the thesis in one sentence: who stops here, how long and why they are not able to charge at home instead.

Map competitors on BEE EVSE records, operator apps and live maps. Something that often is a useful gap in a city is the lack of a reliable charger within about 3 km. On a highway look for gaps closer to 15-25km and remember both carriageways. Two weak chargers next door are worse than one strong independent location. If your power level, uptime or amenity is obviously better, a cluster can still work.

score land terms A revenue share with a mall or fuel dealer often beats a high fixed lease in year one With a fixed rent on a speculative plot, you bear all the utilisation risk. Confirm exclusive rights of charging, parking enforcement, hoarding rights, payment of civil works.

Do a load study with the DISCOM before you sign. Ask for the available capacity, the dedicated EV connection pathway, the estimated cost of the service line and a realistic energisation time. The Metro links are quick. Months for[ new upstream infrastructure](https://spiderenergy.in/blog/india-ev-charging-infrastructure-2026).

Underwrite usage in ranges not a single heroic number. As a rough rule of thumb, industry commentary has suggested many public sites break even at around 10-12 percent utilisation, with far higher occupancy achievable where fleets or dense corridor traffic are contracted. Highway studies have shown that stations operating at about 30 percent utilisation can pay back in a few years, while 5 percent sites may never recover capital. Those [bands are stress tests](https://www.ghar.tv/intelligence/ev-charging-highway-fuel-retail-real-estate-investment-india-2026-returns-risks/artgi215), not promises.

Finally, choose the mix of chargers last. The output of the site thesis should be hardware.

## **How Do I Set Up an EV Charging Station in India?**

Public EV charging is a [de-licensed activity ](https://www.evselect.in/ev-charging-station-setup-india)under guidelines of the Ministry of Power. Public charging station operators do not need to hold an electricity retail licence. You need to adhere to the technical and safety norms of the Central Electricity Authority, use certified equipment and follow the procedures of the state DISCOM.

A practical sequence:

- [ ] **Select and control the site.** Own, lease, or revenue-share parking that can take at least two four-wheeler bays for a starter DC site, more if you plan simultaneous guns. Confirm land-use and parking permissions.
- [ ] **Choose certified hardware.** BIS-aligned chargers under the IS 17017 family, CCS2 for four-wheeler DC fast charging, Type 2 for AC destination charging. Match kW to dwell. Prefer OCPP-capable units so you can change network software later.
- [ ] **Apply to the DISCOM for a dedicated metered EV connection.** Public stations should sit on a separate meter and the EV tariff category where the state has notified one. Priority connection timelines exist in central guidance, but local practice varies with load and network work.
- [ ] **Build upstream and civil works.** Transformer or load enhancement, cables, earthing, canopy, drainage, lighting, bay marking, and protection. Distance from the main electrical panel drives the cost. Keeping runs short is a design rule, not a nicety.
- [ ] **Clear safety inspections.** Higher-load DC sites commonly need Chief Electrical Inspector (CEIG) or state electrical inspectorate approval, plus fire NOC where the building or basement rules require it.
- [ ] **Connect software, payments, and the network.** App start, RFID, and increasingly plug-and-charge. List the site on major discovery apps. Uptime monitoring is part of the asset, not an add-on.
- [ ] **Register and commission.** State nodal agencies and scheme portals may require registration even when no subsidy is claimed. Test connectors, billing, and failovers before opening.
- [ ] **Operate.** Idle fees, bay enforcement, spare-parts response, and a public uptime target decide whether drivers return.

The PM E-Drive operational guidelines provide centralised support for public charging infrastructure with higher support at Government-controlled hubs and upstream support at many mall, market and highway locations. Direct proposal rights are based on government and public sector applicants who could appoint charge-point operators. The scheme is accessed by private investors through those partnerships rather than an automatic cheque. Always check eligibility in real-time before you bake the [subsidy into the model](https://heavyindustries.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-12/lsauq_1511_0.pdf).

## **Risk Analysis for Investors**

The key risks are utilisation risk, grid delay, lease insecurity, equipment downtime and policy or tariff change. Secondary risks: ICE blocking, app fragmentation and overbuilding in a 3 km urban cell with strong competitors .

Mitigation is both contractual and operational. SLA-backed hardware, remote monitoring, revenue-share land deals, fleet anchor agreements and a maintenance reserve do more for IRR than a slightly cheaper imported pedestal. Do not underwrite a site on subsidy only. Underwrite it on the cars already through the gate.”

Helpful in portfolio construction. Pair a DC hub in a corridor with an AC cluster in a destination: daytime highway demand and overnight urban demand aren’t the same.

## **People Also Ask**

### **Q: What makes a great EV charging location?**

A place where drivers stop already, stay long enough for your charger speed, can see and enter the bays easily and the grid can deliver stable power. Amenities and enforcement make a pin on the map into repeat sessions.

### **Q: What makes a great EV charging location in India?**

India has the strongest patterns of DC fast charging at highway plazas, fuel retail and transport hubs, and AC destination charging at malls, IT parks, hotels and housing. Land price is not the only factor to consider. Visibility, DISCOM readiness and a gap versus reliable competitors are equally important.

### **Q: How do I select an EV charging location?**

Develop a demand thesis Map EV density and competitor gaps Match dwell time to AC or DC Confirm electrical capacity with the DISCOM Negotiate land terms that share utilisation risk Then select hardware Do not reverse that order.

### **Q: How do I set up an EV charging station in India?**

Take charge of the site, specify certified chargers, take a dedicated EV metered connection, complete civil and upstream works, electrical and fire clearances as needed, integrate payments and monitoring, register with the nodal process if applicable, commission. Public charging does not require a power licence.

### **Q: Do I need a licence to operate public EV charging?**

No. Central guidelines treat public charging as a de-licensed activity. CEA safety standards, certified equipment, and DISCOM connection rules still apply.

### **Q: Which locations usually pay back faster?**

Busy highway and fuel-retail DC sites with amenities and decent uptime are often modelled with shorter paybacks than isolated urban AC points. Actual results depend on sessions, tariff, and power cost. Thin utilisation can prevent payback on any format.

### **Q: What subsidies exist for charging stations?**

PM E-Drive includes support for public charging infrastructure, with the highest combined support on specified government-controlled sites and upstream support at many mall and highway locations. Application routes favour government and public-sector entities that may work with operators. Confirm current windows and categories before relying on them.
