Tata’s charging-support material puts 3.3 kW AC in the slow home charging group, and Tata’s guide says[ 7.2 kW](https://ev.tatamotors.com/punch/ev.html) is about twice as fast as 3.3 kW. A 7.4 kW home charger makes sense when the vehicle officially supports it and the owner is looking for shorter charging windows. On Hyundai’s page for the CRETA Electric, it mentions 7.4 kW AC charging in about 6 hours. This is not about niche figures. They’re the difference between “charge overnight” and “top up fast enough to matter.”

### **Buying tips for Telangana & Andhra Pradesh homes in 2026**

First, ensure that the charger is compatible with the vehicle’s official AC charging support. In most cases, 3.3 kW is enough for an EV if it is a compact city car. If the car officially supports 7.4 kW and the owner wants shorter charging windows then the stronger charger is worth it. The simplest rule for buying. You should check the details on the official pages for MG, Tata and Hyundai. They have model specific charging behaviour and that should dictate your install decision.

Second, look at the parking situation before you buy. SpiderEV says its Har Ghar home charging is for homes, apartment dwellers, offices, restaurants, small businesses and parking lots. Which means the physical location is as important as the EV. A bad install is a bad install, if the parking bay is awkward, the cable route is long or the home association is not ready, the best charger on paper will still be a bad install.

Third, select a charger that is BIS-certified, OCPP-compliant and for Indian grid conditions. SpiderEV literally says that on its main page. In Telangana and Andhra Pradesh, local support is important because quick service can be the difference between a charger that continues to work and one that becomes a problem.

4. Remember, the charging time is not a fixed value. Hyundai states that the charging time depends on the charger specification, the ambient temperature and the power supply being available at all times. This is a useful warning for homes in hot climates and for any installation which depends on a steady electrical supply.

### **What not to do**

Don’t buy a 7.4 kW charger just because it sounds fancy. The car has to carry it, and the home has to justify it. Tata’s charging pages show that some EVs are very comfortable on 3.3 kW, while others really do benefit from 7.2 kW. The charger should trail the car, not the other way around.

Don’t assume all EVs need the same charger tier.  The [MG Comet EV](https://www.mgmotor.co.in/vehicles/comet-ev-electric-car-in-india) is a very different home charging proposition as compared to the Hyundai CRETA Electric. The Windsor EV and Nexon EV are also in the stronger-AC-charging category, while the Tiago EV is a tidier overnight-AC case. The disconnect is people buy the charger first and think later. That's weak procurement.

Don't overlook local support. SpiderEV, which is building and installing infrastructure in Telangana and Andhra Pradesh, says local support is faster because hardware is made in India. The purchase includes serviceability if the charger will be located in a house in Hyderabad, Vijayawada, Visakhapatnam or elsewhere in the two states.

## **People Also Ask**

### **1) Is a 3.3 kW home charger enough for most EVs in Telangana & AP?**

Yes, it is sufficient for many buyers. A good example is MG’s Comet EV which supports 3.3 kW AC charging and takes around 7 hours to go from 0-100% on that setup. The Tata Tiago EV is also clearly in the 3.3 kW-first lane, with the company listing a 3.3 kW AC charger box on the official specs page. If the vehicle is parked overnight, 3.3 kW is often the best and most economical home option. It is slower than 7.4 kW but that is not a flaw if the car is parked for long hours.

### **2) When should I choose a 7.4 kW home charger instead?**

7.4 kW if the car officially supports it and you need a quicker turnaround. Hyundai's CRETA Electric takes about 6 hours for 7.4 kW AC fast home charging, MG's Windsor EV shows 7.4 kW AC home charging in about 6.5 hours, and Tata's Nexon EV shows 7.2 kW AC wallbox charging in 6 hours 36 minutes. All those are cases where it is clear which is the better charger. 7.4 kW is better home wallbox for larger battery models in your EV, or business cars that need to go back out sooner.

### **3) Which home charger is best for the MG Comet EV?**

The simplest default is a 3.3 kW charger and the Spider Mini 3.3 kW or Spider Lite 3.3 kW are a good match for that use case. MG says the Comet EV will do 3.3 kW home charging in about 7 hours and 7.4 kW in about 3.5 hours, so a faster wallbox is also reasonable if the owner wants to cut the wait time. 3.3 kW is good enough for most Comet EV owners in Telangana or Andhra Pradesh, unless the vehicle sees very high turnover.

### **4) Which home charger is best for the Tata Nexon EV?**

The cleaner answer is Spider Smart 7.4 kW.  Tata says the Nexon EV can be charged using 3.3kW AC, 7.2kW AC fast charging and home wallbox charging. With 7.2kW, it takes 6 hours and 36 minutes to charge from 10-100%. If you want a faster AC home charger, this makes the Nexon EV one of the best options. If you’re on a shoestring budget or wiring, then 3.3 kW still works but 7.4 kW is the better long-term fit for many Nexon EV buyers.

### **5) What is the best home charger for the MG Windsor EV?**

Spider Smart 7.4 kW is the best fit. MG says the Windsor EV takes around 6.5 hours on a 7.4 kW AC home charger, and around 13.8 hours on a 3.3 kW portable charger. If you want practical overnight charging, MG’s own data makes the choice clear: the car is much happier on 7.4 kW. This makes it a strong home-charger candidate for Hyderabad, Vijayawada, Visakhapatnam and other use-cases in the city.

### **6) Can apartment residents in Telangana or Andhra Pradesh install home chargers?**

Yes, if the parking and the electrical works. Homeowners, offices, restaurants, small businesses and parking lots are explicitly part of the Har Ghar push of SpiderEV, and so are apartment dwellers. In practice, independent service helps but flat installs require cleaner cable routing and better coordination.

Yes.  Hyundai’s official pages show that 7.4 kW AC fast home charging takes about 6hours, with a portable charging cable option.
