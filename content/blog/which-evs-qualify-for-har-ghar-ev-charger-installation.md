# **Which EVs Qualify for Har Ghar EV Charger Installation in India**

In short: Any EV with an AC charging inlet and onboard charging capability can be configured with the Har Ghar home charger, assuming the home site is able to support the installation. The current Har Ghar program is based on 3.3 kW single phase home chargers and uses standard Indian home electrical connections, so the main filter is [charger compatibility](https://www.mgmotor.co.in/blogs/types-of-chargers) and not brand recognition.

That’s where folks usually get lost. They think installing a home charger is about a car being “premium” or “new enough.” No. It’s about whether the EV can charge at home with AC, what connector it has, and whether the electrical setup can take the charger size. SpiderEV’s AC charger range page says the home lineup has 3.3 kW home chargers and more powerful AC units like 7.4 kW. The[ home models ](https://spiderenergy.in/har-ghar)are designed for standard residential use, the page says.

## **What “qualifies” actually means**

A vehicle qualifies if three things come together.

First, it must be able to charge from AC. Second, it needs either a compatible charging inlet or a home-charging method approved by the manufacturer. Thirdly, the home installation must be compatible with the vehicle’s charging behaviour and the electrical capacity available. This is the working definition of eligibility. According to the Har Ghar page, the system has a 3.3 kW charger and a regular home connection, which is great for EVs that are charged at home overnight rather than needing a fast-charging-type setup.

### **Table 1: Har Ghar qualification logic**

| **Filter** | **What must be true** | **Why it matters** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Vehicle charging type** | The EV must support **AC home charging**. | The current Har Ghar install is a home AC setup, not a DC fast charger. |
| **Connector compatibility** | The vehicle must accept a matching inlet or approved charging method. | If the connector and vehicle do not match, the install is useless. |
| **Home electrical readiness** | The site must support a standard home electrical connection and safe installation. | The Har Ghar page says the program is built for standard home connections. |
| **Charging expectation** | The owner must be fine with overnight or low-speed home charging. | The default Har Ghar chargers are **3.3 kW**, so this is not a rapid-charge system. |
| **Usage pattern** | The car should be used in a way that makes home charging useful. | Home charging works best when the vehicle sits parked for long hours. |

The first filter is simple. If the EV can charge on AC at home, it’s a good bet to fit the Har Ghar model. If the EV only supports fast DC charging, then home installation is still possible for overnight use, but that’s not the point of the program.

## **Which EVs qualify most easily under the current 3.3 kW home setup**

The easiest EVs for the present Har Ghar arrangement are the ones that are fine with slow AC charging at home. SpiderEV’s Har Ghar page lists the current home models as Spider Mini (3.3 kW) and Spider Lite (3.3 kW), both of which are single-phase chargers that are suitable for standard home connections. That means compact city EVs and mainstream EVs that don’t need a high-power charger to be practical are the cleanest fit.

To clarify, if the EV can be overnight charged and the owner is happy with a slow, steady top-up, it qualifies cleanly. The most obvious example is MG’s Comet EV, where the manufacturer has 3.3 kW AC charging right there on the specs page. The same is true for cars that support 7.4 kW AC, because a 3.3 kW wall charger is still a perfectly valid home charging method, it will just be slower.

## **What does not qualify cleanly**

Things that don’t qualify cleanly are not “EVs with a bigger badge.”

These are cars that do not support AC home charging, or owners that expect the home charger to behave like a highway fast charger. The Har Ghar setup is a 3.3 kW home system, not a DC fast charger. The AC charger page says stronger AC units like 7.4 kW, 22 kW and beyond exist for other use cases. So if the owner needs high daily throughput and fast turnaround, the current home program is not the right mental model.

### **Table 3: What does not fit the current Har Ghar expectation**

| **Situation** | **Why it is a problem** |
| --- | --- |
| **No AC charging support** | A home AC charger needs a compatible AC charging path. |
| **Only DC-fast-charging mindset** | Har Ghar is a home AC installation, not a public DC station. |
| **Home electrical setup is too weak or unsafe** | The installation needs a standard home connection and proper site readiness. |
| **Owner expects full-charge speed in a short window** | A 3.3 kW charger is deliberately slow and practical, not rapid. |
| **Connector mismatch** | The vehicle and charger inlet must be compatible. |

Here’s where the hard truth counts. A home charger is no status gadget. It’s a slow energy delivery device. The setup is good if the car needs a top up on the battery overnight. The owner wants the behaviour to be like a petrol refill? Wrong expectation.

## **Which charger power level fits which EV**

Not all EVs require the same home charger. Some cars are happy with 3.3 kW, some are better with 7.4 kW and some can use 11 kW or higher charging on AC if the home and car are both able to support it. [Kia’s home charger page](https://www.kia.com/in/kia-ev-edge/ev-charging-infra/ev-home-charger.html) lists 7.4 kW single phase and 11 kW three phase home charging capabilities. Hyundai’s CRETA Electric is compatible with 7.4 kW AC wallbox charging and mobile home charging. MG’s Comet EV supports 3.3kW and 7.4kW AC charging. That gives you a sense of where the home charging ladder really sits.

### **Table 4: Charger size versus EV use case**

| **Charger size** | **Best for** | **Why** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **3.3 kW** | City EVs, daily commuters, overnight charging. | Lowest home-install friction and matches the current Har Ghar setup. |
| **7.4 kW** | Larger EVs, heavier daily users, faster overnight top-ups. | Better for cars that support stronger AC home charging. |
| **11 kW** | Homes with three-phase capacity and EVs that can use higher AC power. | Faster home charging, but requires better electrical readiness. |

The biggest charger isn't always the right one. The right charger is the one which fits the car, the home wiring and the actual usage pattern of the owner. A 3.3kW charger is usually suitable for a home parked overnight. When the car and the electrical setup can justify it, it makes more sense to have a 7.4 kW charger.

## **The property side of eligibility matters too**

The Har Ghar page is not only for private villa owners. It specifically states that the program can be applied to homeowners, shop owners, offices, restaurants, small businesses, parking lots and apartment residents. That is to say, 'qualify' is partly a vehicle question, partly a site question. If the property can accommodate a safe AC install the user is generally free to proceed.

This is important as many EV owners assume only independent houses can install a home charger. That’s not right. The official Har Ghar page includes apartment residents and parking-lot use cases, meaning the eligibility logic is wider than a villa driveway. The real limitation is if the site can safely support the electrical load and installation.

## **Practical qualification checklist for buyers**

And the brochure is not the place to check the car and the house before approving a home charger.

The vehicle must have an AC charging capability, a compatible inlet and an onboard charging system that works with a home wallbox or portable charger. The home should have the right electrical capacity, sufficient safe wall space, a sensible cable route and a layout that allows the charger to be used daily, without friction. The current Har Ghar is clearly a 3.3 kw home AC model so that is the technical baseline to assume.

### **Table 5: Qualification checklist**

| **Checklist item** | **Pass condition** | **Why it matters** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **EV supports AC charging** | The car can charge from a home AC unit. | The home installation is AC-based. |
| **Connector compatibility** | The inlet and charger match or are officially supported. | No match means no charge. |
| **Home electrical readiness** | The house can safely support the charger load. | The program uses standard home electrical connections. |
| **Parking access** | The car can be parked close enough to the charger for daily use. | Daily convenience is the real value. |
| **Usage pattern** | The vehicle can sit parked long enough for overnight or slow charging. | 3.3 kW home charging is deliberately slow. |
| **Installation site** | Apartment, villa, office, parking lot, or similar property can host the charger. | Har Ghar explicitly includes these property types. |

All these checks return a positive answer, the EV is qualified. If not, the home charger install should be delayed until the electrical or parking issues are resolved. Since the car is already purchased, people get problems that could have been avoided with a bad install.

## **The clean answer by vehicle category**

Since they’re designed to be charged at home with AC power and used overnight, compact city EVs usually qualify easily. The most obvious is the MG Comet EV, for which the manufacturer lists a Type 2 connection and 3.3 kW AC charging. Mid-range EVs and SUVs with AC home charging support like the MG ZS EV and the Hyundai CRETA Electric are also eligible. Larger family EVs like Kia Carens Clavis EV also qualify when the home is ready for higher-capacity AC charging.

So the most restrictive rule is this – if the EV supports AC charging at home, then it qualifies. The only difference is how quickly it will charge and if the house should remain at 3.3 kW or upgrade to a more powerful wallbox in the future. Therefore, the home charger should be selected after verifying the official charging specifications of the car, not before.

## **FAQs**

### **1) Which EVs qualify for Har Ghar EV charger installation?**

Any EV with an AC home charging compatible charging inlet may qualify. “In the current Har Ghar setup there are 3.3 kW home chargers, on a normal home electrical connection. The vehicle has to be compatible with such charging. Some examples of such EVs are MG Comet EV (Type 2 and 3.3 kW / 7.4 kW AC charging), MG ZS EV (Type 2 + CCS and 7.4 kW AC charging), Hyundai CRETA Electric (CCS2 + 7.4 kW AC wallbox support), Kia Carens Clavis EV (7.4 kW single-phase + 11 kW three-phase home-charging options). Those are samples, not the full list. The practical rule is simple: if the EV supports AC home-charging, it generally qualifies.

### **2) Does a bigger EV battery need a bigger home charger?**

Not always but sometimes. “A bigger battery means more energy has to get into the car so a more powerful AC charger can cut down on charging time. That said the Har Ghar setup currently has 3.3 kW chargers which are okay for overnight charging and daily top ups. The manufacturer also supports 7.4kW or 11kW home charging for cars like the Hyundai CRETA Electric or Kia Carens Clavis EV, which can make sense if the house has the electrical capacity and the owner wants a faster turnaround. The right way is to match the charger to the official AC support of the car, and to the wiring in the house, not to guess based on the size of the battery.

### **3) Can apartment residents qualify for Har Ghar installation?**

Yes.  The Har Ghar page makes it clear that the program is open not just to apartment dwellers but also to homeowners, shop owners, offices, restaurants, small businesses and parking lots. That means the property type is not limited to independent houses. The real question is, can the site safely support the installation, and does the vehicle itself support AC charging. In practice, putting a charger in a flat works on the same basic logic as any other site: power available, access for cars, and an EV that can use it. Yes flat dwellers can qualify, but the installation still has to pass the site check.

### **4) Can an EV that only supports DC fast charging qualify?**

Not in the usual sense of the term. Har Ghar is a home AC charger program with 3.3 kW capacity single-phase chargers. This means the vehicle must be able to use AC home charging. DC fast charging is a totally different use case and is part of public or corridor charging, not a home plug and play install. The best fit would be vehicles that can charge both AC and DC . If a model does not have a practical AC home charging route, it does not fit neatly into the Har Ghar model.

### **5) Is a 3.3 kW home charger enough for most EVs?**

Yes, this is more than enough for a lot of people. Home charging is generally an overnight activity. On the official spec page, MG’s Comet EV lists 3.3 kW AC charging, pointing out that some EVs are built with this exact use case in mind. The 3.3 kW Spider Mini and Spider Lite are used for the Har Ghar program. This goes on to show that the present home model is intended to be practical, not quick. Usually 3.3 kW is enough if the driver parks for long hours and needs a simple home solution. If the owner wants a quicker turnaround, or has a larger EV, a more powerful home AC charger can be considered down the road, if the vehicle and electrical setup allow for it.

### **6) What charger type do I need for a Type 2 EV?**

If your EV has a Type 2 AC inlet then a compatible home AC charger is a good place to start. MG Comet EV lists Type 2 connection, and MG ZS EV lists Type 2 plus CCS; both have clear AC home-charging support.  The badge on the car does not matter, it is the charging interface and the onboard charger in the car that matters. A Type 2 compatible EV generally plays well with a home AC charger, particularly if the home setup is a typical residential installation. The shape of the plug alone will not determine the charge rate, but also the vehicle’s onboard charger capacity and the power of the wallbox.

### **7) Do luxury EVs qualify, or only small EVs?**

Both of them can, if they support AC home charging. The current Har Ghar model is not restricted to low-cost EVs, but is restricted by charger compatibility and site readiness. The MG Comet EV is a compact city EV that will easily qualify for Type 2 AC charging and 3.3 kW home charging. The official spec sheet also mentions a larger EV like the Hyundai CRETA Electric with support for 7.4 kW AC Wallbox charger and portable home charging. So the real division isn’t luxury versus budget. Support vs. no support for AC home charging.

### **8) What is the smartest next step if I want to install Har Ghar for a new EV?**

Check the EV's official charging spec first, then check the site. This means checking whether the vehicle supports AC home charging, which connector it uses and whether the home can safely support a 3.3 kW installation or a more powerful AC wallbox at a later stage. As per the Har Ghar page, the process starts with registration and site verification, while the AC charger page states that the home lineup includes 3.3 kW and 7.4 kW models. For the cleanest path, pick an EV with official home-charging support, and let the installation follow the vehicle, not the other way around. That’s the safest way of avoiding a mismatch.
