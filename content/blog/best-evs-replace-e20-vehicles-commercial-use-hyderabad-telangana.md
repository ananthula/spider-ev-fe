# **Best EVs to Replace E20 Vehicles for Commercial Use in Hyderabad & Telangana**

Hyderabad and Telangana's commercial EV adoption is no more a theory. Telangana’s clean-energy policy specifically includes EV charging stations including battery swapping facilities. Its solar/EV infrastructure page says the state will support nighttime community parking with charging for e-autos, shared mobility taxis and public transport vehicles in industrial zones.[ TGREDCO also points out that Hyderabad has 111 public charging stations ](https://tgredco.telangana.gov.in/Updates_2025/Telangana_Clean_and_Green_Energy_Policy_2025.pdf)and says it is moving ahead with large charging-station O&M works in GHMC limits. That’s important because commercial EVs only work [when the charging layer is visible](https://tgredco.telangana.gov.in/Solarenergyprogram.aspx), accessible and functional.

According to its live locator, [SpiderEV’s AP/Telangana network ](https://spiderenergy.in/ev-charging-station-app)covers Hyderabad, Vijayawada and Visakhapatnam with[ real-time station availability](https://spiderenergy.in/ev-charging-station-locator?) and 5,000+ active charging points across 15+ cities. That is the practical baseline for fleet planning in Hyderabad and Telangana. Now you can plan EV usage around actual infrastructure, not wishful thinking. The simple hard truth is. If your commercial vehicle can’t charge reliably, no matter how good the brochure looks, it’s a bad fleet decision.

The replacement of an E20 vehicle is based on the commercial job. A city taxi requires a different EV than a hotel shuttle. A sales car doesn't need the same EV as a fleet car that runs 250 km a day. The ranking below is based on purchase cost, range, charging speed, commercial uptime, ease of maintenance and Telangana charging fit. AFDC notes that electric vehicles[ require less maintenance](https://afdc.energy.gov/vehicles/electric-maintenance) because they have fewer moving parts and fluids, and regenerative braking reduces brake wear. That is more important in commercial use than in private use as downtime burns revenue directly.

The ranking is not based on “most premium” or “most popular.” It is based on use-case fit.

Commercially, I looked at five variables: initial price, certified or claimed range, fast-charging speed, daily practicality and whether the vehicle is suitable for the Hyderabad/Telangana charging reality. The vehicle data is taken from the official pages of the manufacturers, and the infrastructure side is from Telangana/SpiderEV. This gives a practical list, not a hype list.

## **1) Tata Tiago EV: the cheapest serious city-fleet move**

If you're mostly doing Hyderabad city routes, staff transport, small deliveries or short taxi work, Tiago EV is the cheapest serious EV on this list. On Tata’s official pages, it starts from around ₹6.99 lakh to ₹7.99 lakh depending on page and variant context. It offers a range of up to 293 km and a RAI-certified full-charge range of 285 km. Tata also claims the faster-charging version can add 100 km in just 18 minutes. This is enough for lots of everyday city work if you charge reasonably.

The[ Tiago EV ](https://ev.tatamotors.com/tiago/ev.html)makes sense, because in the commercial city it’s about cost per kilometre and uptime, not bragging rights. If you want a car that’s easy to park, easy to swap in a fleet and cheap enough to scale, this is what you’re looking at. It’s not the biggest or fastest EV here. This is the one that you can multiply across a fleet without blowing the capital budget. That makes a difference in real fleet terms.

## **2) Tata Punch EV: the compact all-rounder**

If you want a small car that can handle a little rougher roads and mixed usage better than a hatchback, the[ Punch EV](https://ev.tatamotors.com/punch/ev.html) is the better answer. Tata Punch EV official pages show ₹9.69 lakh price. 40 kWh battery. 468 km ARAI range. 20-80% charging in 26 mins. In its official press material, Tata also claims a real-world range of around 355 km for the model. This combination makes it easier to deploy for mixed city and outer-city commercial work.

Punch EV is a solid commercial middle ground for Hyderabad and Telangana. It’s more affordable to stack up a business case, so to speak, and has more road presence and more range compared to the Tiago EV. That’s handy if you do airport runs, mixed roads or longer city-to-suburb commutes. If you want a commercial EV that doesn't feel like a taxi with the seats ripped out and more like a proper business vehicle, this is one of the best answers.

## **3) Tata Nexon EV: the safest all-round commercial pick**

If I had to pick one EV for a commercial operator looking for the most balanced answer, it would be the [Nexon EV](https://ev.tatamotors.com/nexon/ev/specifications.html). On Tata’s official specs page, it says that the ARAI-certified range is 489 km and it takes 40 minutes to charge from 10% to 80% on a 60 kW DC charger. Tata lists the Nexon EV on its model page starting from ₹12.49 lakh. That’s a serious balance of range, charging speed and everyday practicality.

This is the one that works for most commercial use cases without getting silly. The Nexon EV makes it possible for a city-based business to go about its work with airport runs, client pick-ups, corporate field visits or mixed suburban driving without the nagging concern of range. It’s also a safer bet than a premium EV for a fleet, because the purchase price is still within reach. That makes it the most powerful "do almost everything decently" commercial EV on this list.

## **4) Tata Tigor EV: the sedan option for chauffeur and executive work**

The[ Tigor EV ](https://ev.tatamotors.com/tigor/ev.html)is for commercial buyers looking for a sedan profile and not a hatchback or an SUV. It begins at ₹12.49 lakh on Tata’s official pages and offers a range of up to 315 km. The Tata support page states that charging to 80% on a regular basis takes approximately 8 hours and 45 minutes on a 15A plug point. Therefore, it is best suited for overnight depot or home-style charging rather than rapid turnaround.

From a commercial point of view that makes sense for chauffeur work, corporate transport and city travel with clients. If you need boot space, formal looks and reliable urban performance over SUV styling, a sedan can still be the right tool for your business. The Tigor EV is not the flashiest of the bunch here. It is the cleaner sedan replacement for the sort of E20 petrol cars still doing admin, visitor or field-sales work.

**5) MG Windsor EV: comfort-first urban shuttle**

If your business values rear-seat comfort and urban shuttle behaviour, the [MG Windsor EV ](https://www.mgmotor.co.in/vehicles/windsor-ev-electric-car-in-india)should be on your radar. Official MG page states a range of 331 km to 449 km, AC charging at 7.4 kW for about 6.5 hours and DC fast charging at 45–60 kW for 45–50 minutes. MG’s site also currently lists BaaS-linked pricing options, which can help bring down the upfront entry point depending on how you structure the deal.

It works well for ride-hailing, premium city commuting, family business transport and urban shuttle duty where the cabin is as important as the drivetrain. If you want a vehicle that is less utilitarian than Tiago or Punch and more comfort-oriented then the Windsor EV becomes interesting. The trade-off is that it is more comfort focused than cost minimising, so it makes sense when passenger experience is part of the commercial equation.

**6) Hyundai CRETA Electric: premium executive and airport work**

CRETA Electric is the premium choice for businesses that care about status, comfort and longer range. On Hyundai's official pages you can find 42 kWh and 51.4 kWh battery packs, up to 510 km range and 7.4 kW AC charging. Hyundai’s brochure mentions a 39-minute DC fast charge time. The present Hyderabad pricing page shows the [CRETA Electric starting](https://www.hyundai.com/in/en/find-a-car/creta-electric/specification) at ₹18.02 lakh. Hyundai also announced a BaaS offering in 2026 with lower upfront entry costs. But for commercial fleet planning purposes the standard purchase spec is the cleaner comparison.

It is the top end commercial EV for hotel cars, airport transfers, executive pickups and premium client transportation in the current list in Hyderabad. It’s expensive, but you get range and a stronger premium image. That only makes sense if the vehicle makes enough to justify the capex. It could be the right move for a business that relies on how clients perceive it. That’s a lot of cars for a pure cost-cutting fleet.

Its energy policy page says Telangana will support charging for e-autos, shared mobility taxis and public transport vehicles in industrial-zone night parking.” That is a direct indicator of commercial use. ## **What commercial operators should avoid**

Don’t buy a premium EV on a route that never uses its range or comfort advantages. Waste is that. The CRETA Electric makes sense if the car is customer-facing, premium. Overkill if the vehicle is going to be doing short intra-city tasks its whole life. The same reasoning applies to the Windsor EV. It is a comfort product first and a cost minimiser second. The right vehicle is the one that fits the route, not the one with the biggest launch campaign. That's an inference based on the official range / charging data and the business use-cases above.

And don’t assume a big-battery EV is the best choice for the fleet either. Commercial value is more than simply a range. It's down time, charging convenience, maintenance and route fit. AFDC’s maintenance guidance is helpful here: EVs do cut back on routine maintenance since they have fewer moving parts and fluids, but the business still has to manage charging and uptime carefully. That’s why the Hyderabad/Telangana public charging growth is so crucial.
